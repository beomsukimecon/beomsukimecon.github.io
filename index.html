Work in Progress

Manufacturer Entry and Adverse Selection: Evidence from Certified Used Cars in Korea

Abstract

What happens when manufacturers enter secondary markets with asymmetric information? I study the 2023 entry of Hyundai and Kia into the Korean used-car market through certified pre-owned programs. Entry was initially subject to procurement and disposal restrictions that were lifted in 2025, which I use as variation in the scale of manufacturer inventory. Combining vehicle-level transaction data with a random-coefficients demand model and Bertrand pricing, I ask whether manufacturer certification mitigates adverse selection or mainly reallocates high-quality cars away from independent dealers, and decompose the welfare effects of entry into competition, information, and selection channels.

The Full Price of Electric Driving: Energy Costs, Charging Constraints, and Vehicle Utilization

Abstract

Using trip-level connected-car data from Hyundai and Kia, I estimate how energy prices and refueling constraints shape driving for internal-combustion and electric vehicles. I decompose the response to fuel prices into trip frequency (extensive margin) and distance per trip (intensive margin), and exploit Korea's mandate requiring EV chargers in existing apartment complexes to study charging infrastructure as a non-price constraint on EV use. I use the estimates to compare engineering and utilization-adjusted emissions reductions from electrification.

Where to Put a New Technology: Hybrid Variants and Product-Line Design in the Korean Car Market

Abstract

Multiproduct firms can launch a new powertrain as a variant of an existing nameplate or as a new, dedicated model. I study this choice for hybrid and electric vehicles in Korea, separating cost-side scope economies from platform and powertrain sharing from demand-side internalization of cannibalization, using a static model of differentiated-products demand and multiproduct pricing.

Taxing Sugar after the Zero-Sugar Boom: Vertical Pricing, Product Portfolios, and Reformulation

Abstract

Korea is considering a tiered sugar-sweetened beverage levy in a market where zero-sugar line extensions are already widespread. I estimate demand and vertical pricing for carbonated drinks and simulate the proposed levy, separating how product availability and joint ownership of regular and zero-sugar variants change pass-through, sugar purchases, consumer surplus, and profits.

Agglomeration in the Gangnam Aesthetic Surgery Cluster

with Yutec Sun

Abstract

Using a clinic-level panel of medical providers in Seoul, we document the concentration of aesthetic surgery clinics in Gangnam, decompose co-agglomeration across specialties, and study entry and exit dynamics. We find that demand externalities appear to be internalized within combined clinics rather than across separate ones.
